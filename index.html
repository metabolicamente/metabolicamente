Why metabolism matters

Metabolic psychiatry

We stopped thinking about the root of the problem.

Psychiatry measures your neurotransmitter imbalance and treats the imbalance. The question left out is the only one that matters.

What caused the imbalance?

What is metabolic psychiatry?

An approach that treats mental health as a result of brain metabolism, integrating biological processes into the treatment of psychiatric disorders instead of managing symptoms alone.

The brain is about two percent of your body weight and burns roughly twenty percent of your energy. It is the most metabolically expensive organ you have. When the cells that supply that energy stop working properly, the effects show up as mood, focus, sleep, and thought. They are the things psychiatry has always measured, arriving from a direction it rarely looks at.

What the old model leaves open

Neurotransmitters are real. They are just not the beginning of the story.

Low serotonin, high dopamine, adjust the dose. That explanation is not false, and the medicines built on it relieve real suffering every day. It answers one question and leaves the next one wide open: what pushed the neurotransmitters out of balance in the first place?

01

Many people do not respond

A large share of people move through two, three, four medications without reaching sustained remission. When that happens this often, the assumption underneath is worth re-examining.

02

The diagnoses blur

Anxiety, depression, ADHD and bipolar disorder turn up together in the same person far more often than separate categories would predict. Overlapping symptoms usually point to a shared mechanism.

03

The body comes along

People with serious mental illness carry much higher rates of obesity, type 2 diabetes and heart disease. That gets filed as a side effect. It may be part of the same picture.

Everything runs through metabolism

The known causes of mental illness do not act independently.

How an energy shortage becomes a symptom

Four links, each measured on its own. What is new is reading them as one circle.
01

Mitochondria

The power plants inside the cell

Every neuron carries hundreds to thousands of them. They generate the cell's energy, and they also help build serotonin, dopamine and cortisol, regulate inflammation, and decide when a cell repairs itself.

Mitochondrial dysfunction has been documented in nearly every diagnosis psychiatry treats.

02

Insulin resistance

Energy that exists and cannot get in

Insulin is the key that opens the cell to glucose. When the lock stiffens the body just makes more keys, so fasting glucose looks normal for years while insulin climbs in silence. It does not show on the outside.

Glucose in the prediabetic range carries 2.7 times the risk of major depression.

03

Inflammation

The fire that never quite goes out

Excess glucose sticks to proteins, fats and DNA inside the cell, leaving debris the body cannot recycle. That feeds oxidation, which damages mitochondria, which make less energy, which leaves the cell with less to put the fire out.

The usual fuel is ultra-processed food, poor sleep, alcohol and chronic stress.

04

Circadian rhythm

The clock that sets the rest

Mitochondria follow the body clock. Energy output drops at night so repair can happen, and climbs in the morning. Morning light sets that clock, screens at two in the morning unset it.

It is why mood often lifts across the day and why episodes return with the seasons.

None of the four is the beginning. Inflammation damages mitochondria, and tired mitochondria raise inflammation. Poor sleep worsens insulin resistance, and insulin resistance ruins sleep. That is why a single intervention rarely breaks the circle, and why entering it at several points at once can turn it the other way.

What the treatment actually looks like

Five stages, from first assessment to ongoing care.
01

Initial assessment

You complete a detailed screening covering clinical history, symptoms, eating habits, and lifestyle. Your first appointment happens within 5 days.

  • 10-minute online screening
  • Full history-taking with a psychiatrist
  • Definition of clinical goals
02

Metabolic investigation

We order specific lab tests to map the inflammatory, glycemic, hormonal, and nutritional markers relevant to mental health.

  • Complete metabolic panel
  • Vitamins, minerals, and omega-3
  • Inflammatory markers (CRP, homocysteine)
03

Personalized therapeutic plan

We combine metabolic, nutritional and, when indicated, pharmacological interventions to treat the root of the imbalance, not just the symptoms.

  • Individualized protocol
  • Meal plan guided by your dietitian
  • Medication management
04

Ongoing clinical nutrition

Your functional dietitian follows you weekly, adjusting the plan based on labs, symptoms, and adherence. Everything lives in your patient dashboard.

  • Meal plans updated every two weeks
  • Direct chat with your dietitian
  • Recipes and shopping lists
05

Monitoring and progress

Every 30–60 days we re-evaluate labs and subjective metrics (mood, energy, sleep). You track your progress visually in the dashboard.

  • Progress dashboard
  • Comparative lab reports
  • Guided weekly check-ins

Psychiatry Research · 2024

Ketogenic Diet Intervention on Metabolic and Psychiatric Health in Bipolar and Schizophrenia: A Pilot Trial

Shebani Sethi, Diane Wakeham, Terence Ketter, Farnaz Hooshmand, Julia Bjornstad, Blair Richards, Eric Westman, Ronald M. Krauss, Laura Saslow

This Stanford Medicine pilot study investigated the effects of a ketogenic diet in patients with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia who were already on medication. Over 4 months, 100% of participants showed significant improvement in psychiatric symptoms. Notably, 43% achieved full clinical remission and 64% were discharged on a reduced medication load. Beyond the psychiatric gains, participants showed substantial metabolic benefits, including reductions in weight, waist circumference, and insulin resistance.

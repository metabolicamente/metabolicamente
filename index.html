Metabolic Psychiatry

We stopped thinking about the root of the problem.

Psychiatry measures your neurotransmitter imbalance and treats the imbalance. But the question left out is the only one that matters:

What caused the imbalance?

Maybe the energy is not getting through.

Your brain is 2% of your weight and burns 20% of your energy. It is the most expensive organ you have. When metabolism is compromised, the neuron cannot produce the energy it needs, and the brain starts to fail.

Insulin resistance

Insulin is what lets a cell turn glucose into energy. When blood insulin runs high for years, less of it reaches the brain. The glucose is still there. The cell just cannot use it. A brain like this can be swimming in glucose and still be starving.

Inflammation and oxidative stress

Defence is normal and necessary. When it turns chronic, it wears down the architecture of the brain instead of protecting it.

Some areas of the brain get too slow. Others get too fast. Some stop.

This is what symptoms are made of.

What matters is treating the root.

Therapy and medication matter, and nothing here asks you to give them up. What they cannot do is replace a healthy metabolism. Your mental health depends on the health of your brain.

Your brain is built out of what you eat.

Serotonin, dopamine and the rest are assembled from amino acids, vitamins and minerals, and all of it arrives through food. Food is not the advice at the end of the appointment. It is raw material, and it is fuel.

An imbalance has to start somewhere.

Changing your mind with diet requires changing your mind about diet.

Georgia Ede

Have you ever considered treating yourself with nutritional therapy?

A brain-healthy diet must:

01

Nourish

Provide every essential nutrient the brain uses to build and maintain itself.

02

Protect

Leave out what drives inflammation and oxidative stress.

03

Energize

Keep glucose and insulin in a healthy range, for life.

In ketosis, the brain chooses.

Ketones are not an emergency fuel. There is always plenty of glucose in the blood, and even so, when ketones are available the brain keeps burning ketones.

Less insulin.Ketones burn best exactly when insulin is low.
A shorter path.Glucose takes thirteen chemical reactions to become energy. Ketones take three.
Less oxidation.Burning ketones produces less oxidative stress than burning glucose.

It is a path, not a leap.

You start wider: real food, less carbohydrate, a few weeks. If your labs and your symptoms ask for more, you tighten. If they do not, you stop there.

The goal was never the strictest diet. It is the least restrictive one that works for you.

And you do not do this alone

A drop in carbohydrate moves blood sugar and blood pressure, and it can change the dose you need. That is why a dietitian and a psychiatrist sit on the same side of the table, from the first appointment.

Scientific articles

Psychiatry Research · 2024

Ketogenic Diet Intervention on Metabolic and Psychiatric Health in Bipolar and Schizophrenia: A Pilot Trial

Shebani Sethi, Diane Wakeham, Terence Ketter, Farnaz Hooshmand, Julia Bjornstad, Blair Richards, Eric Westman, Ronald M. Krauss, Laura Saslow

This Stanford Medicine pilot study investigated the effects of a ketogenic diet in patients with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia who were already on medication. Over 4 months, 100% of participants showed significant improvement in psychiatric symptoms. Notably, 43% achieved full clinical remission and 64% were discharged on a reduced medication load. Beyond the psychiatric gains, participants showed substantial metabolic benefits, including reductions in weight, waist circumference, and insulin resistance.

Frontiers in Nutrition · 2024

The Effects of Ketogenic Metabolic Therapy on Mental Health and Metabolic Outcomes in Schizophrenia and Bipolar Disorder

Calogero Longhitano, Sabine Finlay, Isabella Peachey, Jaymee-Leigh Swift, Flavia Fayet-Moore, Toby Bartle, Gideon Vos, Donna Rudd, Omer Shareef, Shaileigh Gordon, Shebani Sethi, Christopher Palmer, Iain Campbell

This paper describes the protocol of a randomized controlled trial investigating the effects of ketogenic metabolic therapy on mental health and metabolic outcomes in patients with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. The study uses a modified ketogenic diet as its main intervention, with multidisciplinary follow-up.

Frontiers in Psychiatry — Contributing Authors

Georgia Ede

M.D. — Nutritional Psychiatrist
Harvard Medical School

A Harvard-trained psychiatrist and nutrition specialist, Dr. Georgia Ede is a pioneer in studying how specific diets affect brain chemistry. Author of 'Change Your Diet, Change Your Mind,' she researches the impact of ultra-processed foods, refined carbohydrates, and nutritional deficiencies on mental disorders. Her work helps ground clinical protocols for dietary intervention in psychiatry.

Chris Palmer

M.D. — Psychiatrist, Researcher
Harvard Medical School

Author of the best-seller 'Brain Energy,' Dr. Chris Palmer is a professor of psychiatry at Harvard and one of the leading advocates of the metabolic theory of mental illness. He proposes that psychiatric disorders are fundamentally disorders of brain metabolism, with the mitochondria at the center of the pathology. His work connects decades of research in neuroscience, metabolism, and psychiatry.

The first question

Do I have to stop my medication?

No, and never on your own. The metabolic approach is complementary to your psychiatric treatment, not a replacement for it. Any change of dose is your psychiatrist's decision, made gradually and only when it is clinically appropriate. Plenty of people improve and stay on medication.

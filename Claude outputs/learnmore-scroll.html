Idea A · Pinned sceneThe drawing holds still, the text steps past it — the drawing changes as you go

01

Maybe the energy is not getting through.

Your brain is 2% of your weight and burns 20% of your energy. It is the most expensive organ you have. When metabolism is compromised, the neuron cannot produce the energy it needs, and the brain starts to fail.

02

Insulin resistance

Insulin is what lets a cell turn glucose into energy. When blood insulin runs high for years, less of it reaches the brain. The glucose is still there. The cell just cannot use it. A brain like this can be swimming in glucose and still be starving.

03

Inflammation and oxidative stress

Defence is normal and necessary. When it turns chronic, it wears down the architecture of the brain instead of protecting it.

04

What matters is treating the root.

Therapy and medication matter, and nothing here asks you to give them up. What they cannot do is replace a healthy metabolism. Your mental health depends on the health of your brain.

How I'd combine them

These are not exclusive. My recommendation, if you want one answer:

  • Width → 1560px. Full-bleed makes long paragraphs hard to read on a wide screen; 1560 kills most of the dead margin and keeps the line length sane.
  • A for the middle of the page. The mechanism section is the one place where a drawing genuinely earns being held on screen while the explanation advances.
  • D everywhere. The rail plus the top progress line is what actually tells someone "you are moving", and it costs almost nothing.
  • B for the two emotional beats — "built out of what you eat" and the closing. Changing the ground colour there does more than any animation.
  • C is the most fun and the riskiest: it fights the reader on a long page and it breaks down on mobile.
